Example 5.1.12.

Consider the vector field \(\vec{F}=\langle -y,x\rangle\) and the curves \(C_{1}: \vec{r}_{1}(t)= \langle\cos (t), \sin (t)\rangle, 0 \leq t \leq \pi \text{,}\) and \(C_{2}: \vec{r}_{2}(t)=\langle 1-2t, 0\rangle, 0 \leq t \leq 1\text{.}\) Note that both curves start at \((1,0)\) and end at \((-1,0)\text{.}\) Verify that \(\int_{C_{1}} \vec{F} \cdot \vec{T} d s \neq \int_{C_{2}} \vec{F} \cdot \vec{T} d s\text{.}\)
🔗
Solution 1.
\begin{gather*} C_1 : \vec r (t) = \langle \cos t , \sin t \rangle , \ 0\leq t \leq \pi , \quad \vec r ' (t) = \langle -\sin t , \cos t \rangle\\ \vec F ( \vec r (t)) = \langle -\sin t, \cos t \rangle , \quad \vec F ( \vec r (t)) \cdot \vec r'(t) = \sin^2 t + \cos^2 t = 1\\ \int_{C_1} \vec F \cdot \vec T \ ds = \int_{C_1} \vec F\ d\vec r =\int_a^b \vec F ( \vec r (t)) \vec r' (t)\ dt = \int_0^{\pi} 1 \ dt =\pi \end{gather*}

🔗
Solution 2.
\begin{gather*} C_2 : \vec l (t) = \langle 1-2t,0 \cos t \rangle , \ 0\leq t 1, \quad \vec r ' (t) = \langle -2 ,0 \rangle\\ \vec F ( \vec r (t)) = \langle 0,1-2 t \rangle , \quad \vec F ( \vec r (t)) \cdot \vec r'(t) = -2\cdot 0 + 0\cdot (1-2t) = 0\\ \int_{C_2} \vec F \cdot \vec T \ ds = \int_{C_2} \vec F\ d\vec r =\int_a^b \vec F ( \vec r (t)) \vec r' (t)\ dt = \int_0^{1} 0\ dt =0 \end{gather*}
