Example 4.2.10.

Evaluate the line integral \(\int_{C}\left(x^{2}-y^{2}\right) d s\text{,}\) where \(C\) is the line segment from \((2,0)\) to \((0,1)\text{.}\)
🔗
Net area bounded by \(f(x, y)\) and the \(x y\)-plane, restricted to \(\vec{r}(t)\)
🔗
Solution.
The line segment from \(P=(2,0)\) to \(Q=(0,1)\) can be parametrized as
\begin{equation*} \vec{r}(t) = \langle 2,0\rangle + t\langle -2,1\rangle = \langle 2-2t,\, t\rangle, \ 0\leq t \leq 1, \ \vec{r}'(t) = \langle -2,1\rangle\text{,} \end{equation*}
so \(x(t)=2-2t\) and \(y(t)=t\text{.}\) Then
\begin{equation*} |\vec{r}'(t)| = \sqrt{(-2)^2+1^2} = \sqrt{5}\text{,} \end{equation*}
so \(ds = \sqrt{5}\, dt\text{.}\) Since
\begin{equation*} x(t)^2 - y(t)^2 = (2-2t)^2 - t^2 = 4-8t+4t^2-t^2 = 4-8t+3t^2\text{,} \end{equation*}
the line integral becomes
\begin{equation*} \int_{C}(x^2-y^2)\, ds = \sqrt{5}\int_0^1 (4-8t+3t^2)\, dt = \sqrt{5}\left. \left(4t-4t^2+t^3\right)\right|_0^1 = \sqrt{5}(4-4+1) = \sqrt{5}\text{.} \end{equation*}
